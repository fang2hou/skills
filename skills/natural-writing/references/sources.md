## 1. English Writing and Style Tradition

### `orwell-1946`

- **Title**: _Politics and the English Language_
- **Author**: George Orwell (Eric Arthur Blair)
- **Year**: 1946 (Published in _Horizon_, Vol. 13, No. 76, April 1946, pp. 252–265)
- **URL**: https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/
- **Core Thesis**: Slovenly language and muddled or insincere thought reinforce each other in a reversible feedback loop. Bad modern prose avoids the labor of choosing words for their meaning by gumming together prefabricated strips of words: (1) _dying metaphors_ that evoke no mental image, (2) _operators or verbal false limbs_ that replace simple verbs with noun-plus-light-verb padding, (3) _pretentious diction_ that dresses biased or banal claims in Latinate/scientific grandeur, and (4) _meaningless words_ lacking concrete referents. Meaning must choose the word rather than pre-arranged phrases choosing the thought.
- **Applicable Scope**: Political commentary, essays, journalism, institutional reports, and general expository prose across languages (via structural equivalents of nominalization, dead imagery, and bureaucratic euphemism).
- **Limitations**: Rule (ii) ("short word over long") and Rule (v) ("Anglo-Saxon over Latin/Greek/scientific") reflect English lexical stratification and fail when an exact scientific, legal, or technical term is required. Rule (iv) ("active over passive") does not apply when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or when patient-topic continuity is needed. Orwell himself added Rule (vi) ("Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous") as an explicit escape hatch against mechanical application.

### `strunk-white-1959`

- **Title**: _The Elements of Style_ (1st ed. 1918/1920 by William Strunk Jr., Harcourt, Brace; revised 1959 with E. B. White, Macmillan)
- **Author**: William Strunk Jr. and E. B. White
- **Year**: 1918 / 1959
- **URL**: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/37134
- **Core Thesis**: Vigorous writing is concise: a sentence should contain no unnecessary words, and a paragraph no unnecessary sentences, for the same reason that a drawing should have no unnecessary lines (Rule 13 in 1918 ed. / Rule 17 in 1959 ed.: "Omit needless words"). Prefer definite, specific, concrete language over general, vague, abstract language (Rule 12/16), and put statements in positive form rather than non-committal double negatives such as _not honest_ or _not important_ (Rule 11/15).
- **Applicable Scope**: General nonfiction, journalism, essays, and business prose.
- **Limitations**: As Geoffrey Pullum and Steven Pinker (`pinker-2014`) note, some grammatical prescriptions in _Elements_ overgeneralize personal taste, and "Put statements in positive form" must not be misread as banning contrastive negation (`not X, but Y`) when correcting a real reader misconception.

### `zinsser-1976`

- **Title**: _On Writing Well: An Informal Guide to Writing Nonfiction_
- **Author**: William Zinsser
- **Year**: 1976 (Harper & Row; 30th Anniversary ed. 2006)
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970304959829349268
- **Core Thesis**: "Clutter is the disease of American writing." Writers suffocate clear thought under pompous prepositions (_with minimal impact on_, _at the present time_), redundant adjective-noun pairs (_personal friend_, _future plans_), and institutional stiffness. Good nonfiction strips every sentence to its cleanest components while preserving the writer's human warmth, curiosity, and voice.
- **Applicable Scope**: Nonfiction essays, memoirs, science and technology journalism, and workplace writing.
- **Limitations**: Zinsser's conversational first-person warmth fits signed essays and journalism but requires restraint in anonymous reference specifications, statutory texts, and clinical protocols.

### `williams-1981`

- **Title**: _Style: Ten Lessons in Clarity and Grace_ (1st ed. 1981, Scott, Foresman; trade edition _Style: Toward Clarity and Grace_, 1990, University of Chicago Press)
- **Author**: Joseph M. Williams
- **Year**: 1981 / 1990
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000003214647
- **Core Thesis**: Readers judge prose clear when grammatical structure aligns with conceptual structure: cast central **characters as grammatical subjects** and their key **actions as verbs** instead of locking actions inside abstract nominalizations (_an evaluation of X by Y_ $\to$ _Y evaluated X_). Build sentence-to-sentence **cohesion** by starting clauses with familiar information ("old before new") and placing complex, weighty new information at the end (**end-focus**).
- **Applicable Scope**: Academic writing, technical documentation, legal and policy analysis, and professional prose.
- **Limitations**: When a nominalization names an established technical concept or refers back to a previous clause as a compact topic ("This **migration**..."), unpacking it into a clause reduces cohesion.

### `pinker-2014`

- **Title**: _The Sense of Style: The Thinking Person's Guide to Writing in the 21st Century_
- **Author**: Steven Pinker
- **Year**: 2014 (Viking / Penguin)
- **URL**: https://stevenpinker.com/publications/sense-style-thinking-persons-guide-writing-21st-century
- **Core Thesis**: Adopts Thomas & Turner's **Classic Style**: prose works like a windowpane through which the writer directs the reader's gaze to objects in the world, rather than narrating the writer's own professional activity (_metadiscourse_ such as "In this section I will discuss..."). Identifies the **Curse of Knowledge** (assuming the reader shares the writer's private context and abbreviations) and **zombie nouns** (nominalizations that erase agency) as primary causes of opaque prose, while debunking purist "bogus rules" through modern corpus linguistics and syntax.
- **Applicable Scope**: Scientific, academic, technical, and public intellectual prose.
- **Limitations**: Classic style's "windowpane" metaphor assumes truth can be pointed at directly; in contested diplomatic, legal, or uncertain evidentiary contexts, explicit epistemic framing and hedges remain necessary.

### `gowers-1954`

- **Title**: _The Complete Plain Words_ (combining _Plain Words_, 1948, and _ABC of Plain Words_, 1951)
- **Author**: Sir Ernest Gowers
- **Year**: 1954 (HMSO)
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/completeplainwor0000gowe
- **Core Thesis**: Written for British civil servants to replace defensive bureaucratic "officialese" with clear, courteous human communication. Good official writing puts itself in the reader's shoes, selects the precise and familiar word over legalistic padding (_prior to_, _in the affirmative_, _acquaint_), and avoids both cold institutional pomposity and inappropriate flippancy.
- **Applicable Scope**: Public administration, customer communication, corporate policies, and institutional correspondence.
- **Limitations**: Addressed to administrative correspondence rather than creative or literary prose.

### `fowler-1926`

- **Title**: _A Dictionary of Modern English Usage_ (and _The King's English_, 1906, with F. G. Fowler)
- **Author**: H. W. Fowler
- **Year**: 1926 (Oxford: Clarendon Press; 2nd ed. rev. by Sir Ernest Gowers, 1965)
- **URL**: https://content.fimsschools.com/academy.fims.org.pk/A%20Dictionary%20of%20Modern%20English%20Usage.pdf
- **Core Thesis**: Champions direct, simple, brief, vigorous, and lucid prose. Diagnoses stylistic vices such as **elegant variation** (replacing a clear repeated noun with strained synonyms to avoid repetition), **abstractitis**, **genteelisms**, and **battered ornaments**.
- **Applicable Scope**: Editorial review, usage questions, and stylistic consistency in English.
- **Limitations**: Early twentieth-century British register norms must be filtered through contemporary usage data; however, his critique of "elegant variation" directly explains why LLMs cycle through strained synonyms (_framework / paradigm / architecture / ecosystem_).

### `plain-language-2010`

- **Title**: _Federal Plain Language Guidelines_ (under the US Plain Writing Act of 2010, Public Law 111-274)
- **Author**: Plain Language Action and Information Network (PLAIN) / U.S. General Services Administration
- **Year**: 2010 (rev. 2011)
- **URL**: https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language
- **Core Thesis**: Public and technical documents must be organized so readers can find what they need, understand it on the first reading, and act on it. Emphasizes addressing the reader directly, using active verbs, keeping one main point per sentence, converting dense conditional prose into clear tables or lists when appropriate, and eliminating archaic legalisms (_heretofore_, _shall_, _pursuant to_).
- **Applicable Scope**: Government notices, user documentation, compliance guides, and operational runbooks.
- **Limitations**: Designed for utilitarian instruction and public comprehension; does not target essayistic nuance or literary cadence.

### `vercel-writing-2026`

- **Title**: _Vercel Writing Guidelines_ (`vercel-labs/writing-guidelines`)
- **Author**: Vercel Labs
- **Year**: 2026
- **URL**: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/writing-guidelines/main/command.md
- **Core Thesis**: Establishes high-signal engineering and developer documentation rules across eleven dimensions: (1) **Planning & content type** (explicit single-job pages declared as `Tutorial`, `How-to`, `Reference`, `Conceptual`, `Troubleshooting`, or `Landing`; user-shaped titles; testable Bloom's taxonomy verb goals; shared openers on multi-audience pages), (2) **Voice & tone** (active voice checked via the "by monkeys" mental test; direct `you` address; imperative steps; ~20-word target; warm contractions; present tense; restricted `we` for deliberate author/vendor actions; no marketing rhetorical questions; and the speech-pace **second-read test**), (3) **Reader-pressuring & filler words** (cutting `easy`, `simple`, `quick` in favor of concrete descriptions like `"one command"` or `"default settings"`, and pruning `very`, `just`, `really`), (4) **Concision** (earning every detail when drafting; replacing weasel words like `significantly`, `typically`, `generally` and vague quantifiers like `near-zero`, `sub-second`, `most requests` with cited figures; replacing cadence metaphor verbs like `moves through`, `lands`, `carries`, `hits` with literal steps), (5) **AI-generated tells** (flagging summary-style recap transitions like `With this setup complete…`, stop-start choppy fragments, spec-sheet voice like `provides` / `is configurable`, cold-open body paragraphs without antecedents, personified artifacts like `hand the browser a URL` / `the token holds`, and reused template framing like `The question most teams face is whether…`), (6) **Tone by content type**, and (7–11) **Headings, structure, lists, code blocks, and placeholders** (sentence-case headings, descriptive subheadings, opening TL;DR paragraph, section summary sentences, first-use acronym expansion and term definition, 2–4 sentence paragraphs, colon-introduced lists for 3+ items, ≤25-line / ≤80-column explained code snippets, and `snake_case` text / `1234567890123` number placeholders).
- **Applicable Scope**: Developer documentation, tutorials, how-to guides, reference manuals, troubleshooting guides, and technical explanations.
- **Limitations**: In `rewrite` and `light-polish` modes on existing texts, `core.fidelity.preserve_voice_and_facts` outranks "Earn every detail" (never delete checkable source figures or scope qualifiers). Direct `you` address, contractions, and TypeScript defaults apply to developer documentation rather than academic papers, incident postmortems, or Japanese/Chinese formal registers. Words like `simple` or `quick` remain valid inside established technical names (_Simple Storage Service_, _quicksort_).

## 2. Chinese Grammar, Rhetoric, and Stylistics Tradition

### `lv-zhu-1952`

- **Title**: 《语法修辞讲话》 (_Lectures on Grammar and Rhetoric_)
- **Author**: 吕叔湘 (Lv Shuxiang) and 朱德熙 (Zhu Dexi)
- **Year**: 1951 (serialized in _People's Daily_) / 1952 (book ed., Kaiming Book Company / China Youth Press)
- **URL**: http://www.linguistics.org.cn/jufayuyi/lsapp/view.php?aid=8
- **Core Thesis**: Organizes practical Chinese editing into six lectures: basic grammar, lexicon, function words (虚字), clause structure, expression, and punctuation. Diagnoses pervasive Chinese prose defects: **mismatch of verb-object and subject-predicate collocations** (动宾/主谓搭配不当), **abuse of empty "omnipotent verbs"** (万能动词/形式动词 such as `加以、进行、作出、予以` that turn crisp verbs into bloated noun objects), **arbitrary word coinage** (生造词), **redundant piling** (堆砌), and **improper omission** (苟简).
- **Applicable Scope**: All modern written Chinese (`zh-CN`), especially official, journalistic, academic, and technical prose.
- **Limitations**: Examples draw from mid-20th-century journalism and official reports, though the underlying syntactic and collocation diagnostics remain foundational for modern Chinese.

### `zhu-dexi-1982`

- **Title**: 《语法讲义》 (_Lectures on Grammar_, 1982) and 《现代汉语语法研究》 (_Studies in Modern Chinese Grammar_, 1980)
- **Author**: 朱德熙 (Zhu Dexi)
- **Year**: 1980 / 1982 (Commercial Press)
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970023484965148613
- **Core Thesis**: Demonstrates that modern Chinese syntax differs fundamentally from Indo-European noun-centered grammar: Chinese verbs and adjectives function directly as subjects and objects (`谓词性成分作主宾语`) without morphological nominalization, subject-predicate clauses frequently serve as predicates (`主谓谓语句`, complementing Li & Thompson's 1976 typology of Chinese as topic-prominent), and clauses cohere through serial verb constructions (`连动式`) and covert syntactic relations rather than heavy subordinating conjunctions.
- **Applicable Scope**: Clause structuring, de-nominalization, and natural sentence rhythm in Chinese.
- **Limitations**: Descriptive grammatical treatise; requires translation into editorial heuristics for writers.

### `wang-li-1943`

- **Title**: 《中国现代语法》 (_Modern Chinese Grammar_, 1943) and 《中国语法理论》 (_Theory of Chinese Grammar_, 1944, collected in 《王力文集》 Vol. 1–2)
- **Author**: 王力 (Wang Li)
- **Year**: 1943 / 1944 (Commercial Press)
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-Ia0000031489
- **Core Thesis**: Systematically analyzes **Europeanized syntax (欧化语法)** in modern Chinese (Part VI of _Modern Chinese Grammar_): (1) expansion of `被` passives beyond adversity contexts into neutral/positive descriptions, (2) overlong pre-nominal `的` modifier chains that delay the head noun, (3) mechanical pluralization with `们`, (4) proliferation of abstract suffixations (`-性, -化, -度`), and (5) explicit formal connectives (`当……的时候`, `如果……的话`, `在……之下`) replacing compact native conditional/temporal flow.
- **Applicable Scope**: Diagnosing and repairing translationese (翻译腔) in Chinese prose and LLM outputs trained heavily on translated corpora.
- **Limitations**: Wang Li noted that moderate Europeanization enriched modern Chinese precision in legal and scientific texts; editors must distinguish useful precision from clumsy syntactic calques.

### `ye-shengtao-1934`

- **Title**: 《文心》 (_The Heart of Writing_, co-authored with 夏丏尊 Xia Mianzun, 1934, Kaiming Book Company) and essays on revising prose (《修改文章》)
- **Author**: 叶圣陶 (Ye Shengtao / Ye Shaojun) and 夏丏尊 (Xia Mianzun)
- **Year**: 1934
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970023484904985742
- **Core Thesis**: Grounds Chinese composition in **辞达 (clarity and exactness of expression)** and the organic connection between earnest spoken language (`语`) and disciplined written prose (`文`). Advocates testing every draft by reading it aloud (`诵读法`) to catch unnatural cadence, hollow套话 (stock formulas), and pretentious posturing, insisting that revising language is revising thought itself.
- **Applicable Scope**: General Chinese prose, education, and editorial self-review.
- **Limitations**: "Writing as you speak" in Ye Shengtao means natural syntax and honest voice—not copying raw spoken fillers, dialectal obscurity, or internet slang into formal writing.

### `zhu-guangqian-1946`

- **Title**: 《谈文学》 (_On Literature_, 1946, Kaiming Book Company, including 〈咬文嚼字〉)
- **Author**: 朱光潜 (Zhu Guangqian)
- **Year**: 1946
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971993809724030264
- **Core Thesis**: Changing a single word alters not only denotation (`直指意义`) but also its associative field and emotional temperature (`联想意义`). Critiques **套板反应 (stock responses / cliché reflexes)**—where a writer reaches for ready-made idioms and theatrical sentimentality (`滥情`) instead of observing the specific nuance of the situation.
- **Applicable Scope**: Lexical precision, metaphor selection, and avoiding stock emotional performance in Chinese.
- **Limitations**: Focused primarily on literary and essayistic aesthetics.

### `yu-kwangchung-1987`

- **Title**: 〈论中文的常态与变态〉 (_On the Normal and the Abnormal in Chinese_)
- **Author**: 余光中 (Yu Kwang-chung)
- **Year**: 1987 (published in _Ming Pao Monthly_, October 1987)
- **URL**: https://bdcl.nccu.edu.tw/uploads/chapter_file/file/649abbd7367376274f2e3a55/5%E6%AF%94%E8%BC%83%E8%A6%96%E9%87%8E%E4%B8%8B%E7%9A%84%E4%BF%AE%E8%BE%AD%E6%80%9D%E7%B6%AD__%E4%BD%99%E5%85%89%E4%B8%AD%E8%AB%96%E7%8F%BE%E4%BB%A3%E4%B8%AD%E6%96%87%E7%9A%84%E6%AD%90%E5%8C%96%E7%8F%BE%E8%B1%A1.pdf
- **Core Thesis**: Distinguishes benign syntactic adaptation from **恶性西化 (malignant Westernization)**. Demonstrates how English-influenced weak verbs (`作出、进行`), abstract noun stacks (`知名度、可读性`), redundant prepositional frames (`关于……方面`, `由于……的缘故`), and passive `被` clauses sap Chinese prose of its concise verbal vitality.
- **Applicable Scope**: Editing modern Chinese essays, translations, journalism, and LLM-generated Chinese text.
- **Limitations**: Some classical cadence preferences reflect literary essay style; in technical documentation, clarity and unambiguous scope take precedence over classical symmetry.

## 3. Japanese Composition, Clarity, and Official Style Tradition

### `tanizaki-1934`

- **Title**: 『文章読本』 (_A Manual of Style / Bunsho Tokuhon_)
- **Author**: 谷崎潤一郎 (Jun'ichiro Tanizaki)
- **Year**: 1934 (Chuo Koronsha; collected in Shinchosha Bunko 『陰翳礼讃・文章読本』, ISBN 978-4-10-100516-4)
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I027486178
- **Core Thesis**: Examines the genius of Japanese syntax against forced Western grammar: Japanese naturally omits understood subjects and pronouns (`私、彼、彼女、それ`), relies on context and cadence, and balances visual density across Kanji, Hiragana, and Katakana. Over-specifying every grammatical argument in imitation of English produces stiff, cluttered prose.
- **Applicable Scope**: Japanese narrative, essays, and natural topic-ellipsis across registers.
- **Limitations**: In technical specifications, contracts, and incident reports, omitting the subject when multiple systems or actors are in play causes dangerous ambiguity (see `kinoshita-1981`).

### `iwabuchi-1960`

- **Title**: 『悪文 — 良き文章のための条件』 (_Bad Writing: Conditions for Good Prose_)
- **Author**: 岩淵悦太郎 (Etsutaro Iwabuchi, ed., Director of the National Language Research Institute)
- **Year**: 1960 (Nihon Hyoron Shinsha)
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000001-I27211000080291773
- **Core Thesis**: Analyzes authentic specimens of defective Japanese prose (_akubun_): overlong sentences that lose syntactic thread (**ねじれ文 / 係り受けの混乱**), ambiguous particle usage (`は` vs `が`, `に`, `で`), unclear pronoun reference, and bureaucratic kango piling.
- **Applicable Scope**: Sentence-level structural diagnosis and syntax repair in Japanese.
- **Limitations**: Focuses on avoiding structural defects rather than rhetorical persuasion.

### `honda-1976`

- **Title**: 『日本語の作文技術』 (_Japanese Composition Techniques_)
- **Author**: 本多勝一 (Katsuichi Honda)
- **Year**: 1976 (Asahi Shimbunsha; Asahi Bunko rev. ed. 2015, ISBN 978-4-02-261845-0)
- **URL**: https://www.books.or.jp/book-details/9784022618450
- **Core Thesis**: Formulates deterministic syntactic rules to prevent ambiguity in head-final Japanese: (1) **Place long/clausal modifiers before short modifiers** (`長い修飾語は前に、短い修飾語は後に`), (2) **Order from broad setting to narrow detail** (`大状況から小状況へ`), (3) **Group tightly bound elements and place commas (`、`) at structural boundaries** where multiple modification paths could arise.
- **Applicable Scope**: All Japanese prose—technical, journalistic, official, and general nonfiction.
- **Limitations**: Mechanical application without reading the sentence rhythm can occasionally conflict with topic fronting; clarity of modification (`係り受けの一義性`) is the deciding test.

### `kinoshita-1981`

- **Title**: 『理科系の作文技術』 (_Writing Techniques for the Sciences_)
- **Author**: 木下是雄 (Koreo Kinoshita)
- **Year**: 1981 (Chuko Shinsho No. 624, Chuo Koronsha, ISBN 978-4-12-100624-0)
- **URL**: https://www.chuko.co.jp/shinsho/1981/09/100624.html
- **Core Thesis**: The gold standard for Japanese technical and analytical writing. Establishes three pillars: (1) **Strict separation of Fact (`事実`) and Opinion/Inference (`意見・推測`)**—never blur what was observed with what is conjectured through vague endings like `〜と思われる`, (2) **Front-load the core conclusion/topic paragraph**, and (3) **Write lean, unambiguous sentences** that state one clear proposition without emotional ornament or padded verb endings (`〜することが可能である` $\to$ `〜できる`).
- **Applicable Scope**: Technical documentation, scientific reports, business proposals, and analytical essays in Japanese.
- **Limitations**: Explicitly targets utilitarian/analytical communication (`仕事の文書`), not poetic or literary ambiguity.

### `bunkacho-2007`

- **Title**: 『敬語の指針』（文化審議会答申） (_Guidelines on Honorifics_)
- **Author**: 文化審議会国語分科会 (Council for Cultural Affairs, Agency for Cultural Affairs, Japan)
- **Year**: 2007 (February 2, 2007)
- **URL**: https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/keigo/index.html
- **Core Thesis**: Reclassifies modern Japanese honorifics into five functional types (尊敬語, 謙譲語I, 謙譲語II/丁重語, 丁寧語, 美化語) and emphasizes **自己表現としての敬語**—choosing honorifics accurately based on relationship, setting, and whether an action actually involves permission or benefit. Specifically critiques **double honorifics (`二重敬語`, e.g., `おっしゃられる`)** and the overuse of **`〜させていただく`** when neither permission from the addressee nor personal benefit is present (`発表させていただきます` $\to$ `発表いたします / 発表します`).
- **Applicable Scope**: Japanese business correspondence, customer communication, UI/help prose, and public announcements.
- **Limitations**: Preserves valid uses of `〜させていただく` when genuine permission or benefit exists (e.g., `資料を拝見させていただきます`).

### `bunkacho-2022`

- **Title**: 『公用文作成の考え方』（文化審議会建議） (_Principles for Drafting Official Documents_)
- **Author**: 文化審議会 (Council for Cultural Affairs, Agency for Cultural Affairs, Japan)
- **Year**: 2022 (January 7, 2022; Cabinet notification January 11, 2022, replacing the 1952 guidelines)
- **URL**: https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/93651301_01.pdf
- **Core Thesis**: Modernizes Japanese public and institutional writing around reader comprehension (**読み手とのコミュニケーション**). Mandates consistent **`です・ます` (敬体)** for external/reader-facing explanations, headings that summarize conclusions, replacing stiff bureaucratic expressions and obscure katakana loanwords with clear everyday Japanese, and breaking long multi-clause sentences into digestible units.
- **Applicable Scope**: Official notices, corporate documentation, policy explanations, and public-facing Japanese prose.
- **Limitations**: Internal legal statutes and court judgments retain specialized conventions outside general public communication.

### `iori-yasashii-2016`

- **Title**: 『やさしい日本語—多文化共生社会へ』 (_Plain Japanese: Toward a Multicultural Society_)
- **Author**: 庵功雄 (Isao Iori)
- **Year**: 2016 (Iwanami Shinsho No. 1617, Iwanami Shoten, ISBN 978-4-00-431617-6)
- **URL**: https://www.books.or.jp/book-details/9784004316176
- **Core Thesis**: Demonstrates how cognitive load in Japanese drops sharply when writers (1) unpack dense Sino-Japanese compound nouns (`サ変動詞/漢語連続`), (2) eliminate double negatives (`〜ないわけではない`) and roundabout passives (`〜と考えられるようになっている`), and (3) keep one predicate proposition per clause (`一文一義` / `1機能1形式`, Chapter 3).
- **Applicable Scope**: Public information, onboarding guides, accessibility-conscious documentation, and clear explanations in Japanese.
- **Limitations**: Expert-to-expert technical documents should retain standard technical kango (`べき等性`, `排他制御`) rather than paraphrasing them into beginner vocabulary.

## 4. Pragmatics, Cognitive Linguistics, and Rhetoric

### `grice-1975`

- **Title**: _Logic and Conversation_ (in _Syntax and Semantics_, Vol. 3: _Speech Acts_, ed. P. Cole & J. L. Morgan, Academic Press, pp. 41–58; reprinted in Grice, _Studies in the Way of Words_, Harvard University Press, 1989)
- **Author**: H. Paul Grice
- **Year**: 1975 (Harvard William James Lectures delivered 1967)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/implicature/
- **Core Thesis**: Human communication is governed by the **Cooperative Principle** and four conversational maxims:
  1. **Maxim of Quantity**: Make your contribution as informative as is required; do not make it more informative than is required.
  2. **Maxim of Quality**: Do not say what you believe to be false or that for which you lack adequate evidence.
  3. **Maxim of Relation**: Be relevant.
  4. **Maxim of Manner**: Avoid obscurity of expression, avoid ambiguity, be brief, and be orderly.
- **Applicable Scope**: Diagnosing why LLM prose feels wrong even when grammatically valid: chatbot preambles and restatement closers violate _Quantity_; inflated significance and unverified claims violate _Quality_; refuting strawman objections violates _Relation_; false `not X, but Y` contrasts and mixed metaphors violate _Manner_.
- **Limitations**: Speakers intentionally flout maxims to generate conversational implicature (irony, understatement, poetic metaphor); editors must preserve deliberate implicatures while removing accidental maxim violations.

### `brown-levinson-1987`

- **Title**: _Politeness: Some Universals in Language Usage_ (Studies in Interactional Sociolinguistics 4, Cambridge University Press, DOI `10.1017/CBO9780511813085`)
- **Author**: Penelope Brown and Stephen C. Levinson
- **Year**: 1987 (first published 1978 in _Questions and Politeness_, ed. E. N. Goody)
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000049-I000003968022
- **Core Thesis**: Models politeness strategies around **Positive Face** (desire for approval/intimacy) and **Negative Face** (desire for autonomy/non-imposition), calibrated by three sociological variables: **Social Distance ($D$)**, **Relative Power ($P$)**, and **Ranking of Imposition ($R$)**.
- **Applicable Scope**: Explains the pragmatic failure of AI expressions like `"我会稳稳地接住你的情绪"` (unsolicited high-intimacy positive politeness that violates high social distance $D$ in professional exchanges) and `"绝绝子 / 狠狠拿捏"` (in-group solidarity slang deployed in formal registers), as well as excessive Japanese keigo (over-indexing $D$ and $P$).
- **Limitations**: Cultural weightings of $D$, $P$, and $R$ differ across English, Chinese, and Japanese; rules must be realized per language (`Layer 2`).

### `lakoff-johnson-1980`

- **Title**: _Metaphors We Live By_ (University of Chicago Press, 1980; 2nd ed. with afterword 2003)
- **Author**: George Lakoff and Mark Johnson
- **Year**: 1980
- **URL**: https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1971993809714826544
- **Core Thesis**: Metaphor is not mere poetic decoration; it is a conceptual mapping from a concrete **Source Domain** to an abstract **Target Domain** (`ARGUMENT IS WAR`, `IDEAS ARE OBJECTS`). A metaphor succeeds only when the structural entailments of the source domain fit the target domain and the communicative frame.
- **Applicable Scope**: Evaluating whether a metaphor in prose clarifies thought or creates cognitive dissonance (for example, mapping a routine technical question onto physical/therapeutic catching in `"稳稳地接住你的问题"`, or colliding incompatible source domains in corporate prose).
- **Limitations**: Conventionalized lexical metaphors (`spend time`, `缓存击穿`) function as standard terms and should not be unpacked unless they clash with surrounding imagery.
