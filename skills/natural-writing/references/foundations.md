## 1. Why Human Principles Outlast AI Symptom Blacklists

Large language models generate text by predicting token sequences that maximize likelihood or reward-model preference across diverse contexts. Because the safest statistical continuation is one that sounds authoritative, symmetrical, and polite without committing to risky specifics, models gravitate toward prefabricated phrases, hedged abstractions, and theatrical transitions.

Wait—human writers under time pressure, institutional conformity, or muddled thinking do the exact same thing. In 1946, forty years before personal computers and eighty years before modern LLMs, George Orwell described bad human prose as:

> "gumming together long strips of words which have already been set in order by someone else, and making the results presentable by sheer humbug." (`orwell-1946`)

When an engineering team treats unnatural AI prose merely by banning surface strings (`delve`, `tapestry`, `不是……而是……`, `稳稳地接住`), two failures occur:

1. **Rapid obsolescence**: Next-generation models shift their lexical distribution while keeping the underlying habit of padding verbs, staging false contrasts, or misjudging social distance.
2. **Collateral damage**: Legitimate rhetorical tools (such as contrastive negation when correcting a real misconception) get banned, or models overcorrect into chatty internet slang (`绝绝子`, `no-brainer`, `ぶっちゃけ`) to avoid sounding formal.

Good writing is not the absence of "AI words." Good writing occurs when **meaning chooses the word**, syntax matches agency, rhetoric serves comprehension, and tone respects the social relationship between writer and reader.

## 2. Deep Study: George Orwell's _Politics and the English Language_ (1946)

### 2.1 Language Corruption and the Thought–Language Feedback Loop

Orwell rejects the fatalist view that language decay is an unstoppable natural process. Instead, he identifies a bidirectional causal loop:

- **Thought corrupts language**: When a writer has no precise meaning, wants to hide an uncomfortable reality, or wishes to sound impressive without evidence, concrete images dissolve into cloudy abstractions.
- **Language corrupts thought**: Ready-made phrases become "a packet of aspirins always at one's elbow." Once a writer surrenders to prefabricated collocations, the phrases "construct your sentences for you—even think your thoughts for you, to a certain extent—and at need they will perform the important service of partially concealing your meaning even from yourself."

This is the exact mechanism behind both human bureaucratic prose and LLM boilerplate: **prefabricated phrasing substitutes for conceptual thought**.

### 2.2 Orwell's Four Diagnostic Categories

Orwell catalogs four specific mechanisms by which writers dodge the labor of prose construction:

| Category                              | Mechanism                                                                                                               | Orwell's Examples                                                                                                  | Modern / Cross-Linguistic Equivalent                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Dying metaphors**                | Neither freshly vivid nor fully dead (lexicalized); pasted in as ready-made strips, often mixing incompatible images.   | _toe the line, ride roughshod over, swan song, Achilles' heel, the Fascist octopus has sung its swan song_         | _tapestries of innovation, North Star metric, 赋能抓手形成闭环, 稳稳地接住你的情绪_                                           |
| **2. Operators (verbal false limbs)** | Replacing simple one-word verbs and conjunctions with a general-purpose light verb + abstract noun/adjective + passive. | _render inoperative, militate against, have the effect of, play a leading part in, exhibit a tendency to, not un-_ | _effectuate an optimization, serves as a cornerstone, 进行深入的探讨, 作出重要的贡献, 〜の実行を行う, 〜することが可能である_ |
| **3. Pretentious diction**            | Dressing simple claims or biased judgments in Latinate, scientific, or pseudo-elevated vocabulary for borrowed weight.  | _phenomenon, element, objective, categorical, utilize, ameliorate, status quo, Weltanschauung_                     | _paradigm shift, synergistic ecosystem, 底层逻辑, 顶层设计, 颗粒度, アグリー, リクワイアメント_                               |
| **4. Meaningless words**              | Words in criticism or politics detached from any discoverable concrete referent, used purely for posture or praise.     | _romantic, plastic, values, vitality, living quality, democracy/fascism (as unanchored praise/abuse)_              | _holistic transformation, empowering experience, 深度赋能, 多元生态, 寄り添うソリューション_                                  |

### 2.3 The _Ecclesiastes_ Experiment: Concreteness vs. Abstraction

Orwell contrasts 49 everyday words from _Ecclesiastes_ 9:11 (_"the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet bread to the wise..."_) with a 38-word modern parody (_"Objective consideration of contemporary phenomena compels the conclusion that success or failure in competitive activities exhibits no tendency to be commensurate with innate capacity..."_).

The parody contains fewer words (38 vs. 49) but 50% more syllables (90 vs. 60), replaces six concrete images (_race, battle, bread, riches, favour, swift/strong_) with one mushy nominalization (_success or failure in competitive activities_), and hides the human observer behind an impersonal operator (_Objective consideration... compels the conclusion_). **Brevity of word count alone does not guarantee clarity; concreteness of imagery and directness of verbs do.**

### 2.4 The Six Questions and Six Rules

Before offering rules, Orwell notes that a scrupulous writer asks six questions of every sentence:

1. What am I trying to say?
2. What words will express it?
3. What image or idiom will make it clearer?
4. Is this image fresh enough to have an effect?
5. Could I put it more shortly?
6. Have I said anything that is avoidably ugly?

When instinct falters, Orwell provides six backstop rules:

- **(i)** Never use a metaphor, simile, or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.
- **(ii)** Never use a long word where a short one will do.
- **(iii)** If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
- **(iv)** Never use the passive where you can use the active.
- **(v)** Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word, or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
- **(vi)** Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

## 3. Answering the Six Core Questions on Orwell

To turn Orwell's essay into an engineering-grade multilingual skill rather than a naive prompt translation, we must answer six structural questions:

### Q1: What specific problem does each rule solve?

- **Rule (i) (No print-worn figures of speech)** solves **conceptual anesthesia and mixed imagery**: when writers grab a stock metaphor, they stop visualizing the referent, producing muddled or self-contradictory entailments.
- **Rule (ii) (Short word over long)** solves **pretentious inflation**: stripping polysyllabic armor (_utilize_ $\to$ _use_, _ameliorate_ $\to$ _improve_) so trivial claims cannot masquerade as profound insights.
- **Rule (iii) (Cut every dispensable word)** solves **verbal false limbs and hedge padding**: removing syntactic scaffolding (_the fact that_, _with respect to_, _in a manner of speaking_) that dilutes information density.
- **Rule (iv) (Active over passive)** solves **evasion of agency**: forcing the sentence to name _who_ did _what_ to _whom_ instead of hiding responsibility behind agentless passives (_mistakes were made_, _a curtailment was effected_).
- **Rule (v) (Everyday word over foreign/jargon)** solves **gatekeeping and false objectivity**: preventing technical or foreign terms from being used merely as status markers when plain words carry the exact meaning.
- **Rule (vi) (Break any rule before writing anything barbarous)** solves **mechanical over-correction**: preventing rules (i)–(v) from turning into a rigid straitjacket that destroys idiom, precision, or natural cadence.

### Q2: Why do these problems impair reader comprehension?

From cognitive psychology and psycholinguistics (`williams-1981`, `pinker-2014`, `iori-yasashii-2016`), bad prose taxes working memory through three bottlenecks:

1. **Syntactic unpacking cost**: Nominalizations and verbal false limbs (_"the implementation of a reduction in latency was achieved by..."_) require the reader to hold abstract noun phrases in working memory while inferring the missing agent and verb.
2. **Referential indeterminacy**: Meaningless words and dying metaphors activate no concrete mental model, forcing the reader to guess which real-world state the writer means.
3. **Pragmatic noise (Gricean violation)**: When a sentence spends 60% of its tokens signaling importance or politeness rather than stating propositions, the signal-to-noise ratio collapses.

### Q3: Which principles transfer cleanly to Chinese (`zh-CN`) and Japanese (`ja`)?

Four underlying principles are universal across English, Chinese, and Japanese:

1. **Meaning chooses the word (辞达 / 一義性)**: Conceptual clarity precedes phrasing in all three languages.
2. **Verbs over verbal false limbs**: English _render inoperative / make contact with_ maps directly to Chinese omnipotent light verbs (`进行优化、加以解决、作出贡献`, `lv-zhu-1952`) and Japanese Sino-Japanese light-verb padding (`実行を行う、検討を実施する、〜することが可能である`, `kinoshita-1981`).
3. **Coherent imagery over stock clichés**: Orwell's critique of dying metaphors maps directly to Zhu Guangqian's critique of _stock responses_ (`套板反应`, `zhu-guangqian-1946`) and modern Chinese corporate/emotional clichés (`抓手、闭环、稳稳接住`).
4. **Plain words over bureaucratic posturing**: Orwell and Gowers map directly to Japan's 2022 _Kouyoubun Sakusei no Kangaekata_ (`bunkacho-2022`) and Lv & Zhu's critique of officialese (`官样文章`).

### Q4: Which rules are bounded by English linguistic structure?

Two of Orwell's rules cannot be copied literally into Chinese or Japanese:

1. **Lexical length & Anglo-Saxon vs. Latin/Greek (Rules ii & v)**:
   - In English, short Germanic words (_use, help, start_) are usually clearer than long Latinate words (_utilize, facilitate, commence_).
   - In **Chinese**, disyllabic words (`使用、协助、启动`) and settled four-character technical compounds (`向下兼容、按需加载、故障隔离`) are often clearer and more rhythmically stable than forced monosyllabic words, and technical English identifiers (`tree-shaking`, `fallback`) are clearer to engineers than coined Chinese calques (`摇树优化`).
   - In **Japanese**, vocabulary is stratified into **Wago (和語)**, **Kango (漢語)**, and **Gairaigo (外来語)**. A two-character Kango (`要件`, `課題`) is shorter and clearer than a long Katakana loanword (`リクワイアメント`, `イシュー`), even though Kango is historically borrowed from Chinese (`bunkacho-2022`).
2. **Active vs. Passive Voice (Rule iv)**:
   - In **English**, the passive requires auxiliary _be_ + past participle and often hides the agent.
   - In **Chinese**, many agentless patient-topic sentences (`配置已经更新`, `代码合并了`) use no `被` marker at all (natural notional passive / 意念被动). What harms Chinese is **unmotivated translationese `被`** (`配置被我们成功地进行了更新`, `wang-li-1943`), not patient-topic sentences.
   - In **Japanese**, intransitive verbs (`自動詞`, e.g., `エラーが発生した`, `修正が完了した`) and natural passives maintain topic continuity and polite neutrality without sounding evasive; what harms Japanese is **translationese inanimate passive chains** (`〜によって…が〜される`, `tanizaki-1934`).

### Q5: What are the legitimate exceptions in literature, academia, and technical prose?

Good writing does **not** eliminate rhetoric or specialized terminology:

- **Technical, scientific, and legal precision**: Terms like _idempotency_ (`幂等`, `べき等性`), _amortized complexity_, _hearsay_, or _debridement_ have exact definitions that everyday words cannot replace without introducing ambiguity.
- **Literary and persuasive rhetoric**: Deliberate parallelism, fresh extended metaphor, irony, understatement (_litotes_), and slow atmospheric cadence are valid artistic choices when they serve the work's aesthetic or emotional intent.
- **Intentional passive / patient-topic focus**: When the patient is the persistent topic of a paragraph ("The token is generated by the auth service, signed with Ed25519, and cached for 15 minutes") or in laboratory methods where the procedure—not the technician—is the subject.

### Q6: How do we operationalize these principles into executable editorial behavior?

Instead of static word bans, an editor or agent executes a **four-step diagnostic loop**:

1. **De-prefabricate**: Identify any phrase chosen as a pre-packaged lump (stock metaphor, light-verb operator, false contrast, chatbot preamble).
2. **Extract the proposition**: Ask Orwell's first question—_What is the sentence actually trying to say? Who does what to whom, under what condition?_
3. **Test against context**: Does the metaphor, contrast, or technical term do real semantic or pragmatic work for this specific audience and genre? If yes, **keep it**.
4. **Rebuild from the verb and agent**: If the phrase is dead weight, rewrite using a concrete subject and a direct verb in the target language's native clause structure, then verify that no facts or nuances were lost.

## 4. Synthesis of the English Prose Tradition

Beyond Orwell, the English rules in `rules/en.yaml` integrate four complementary frameworks:

1. **Characters as Subjects, Actions as Verbs (`williams-1981`)**: Whenever prose feels dense or abstract, inspect the grammatical subject and main verb. If the subject is an abstract nominalization (_"The absence of schema validation..."_) and the verb is an empty copula or operator (_"...led to the occurrence of..."_), restore the actor and action (_"Because the service did not validate schemas, malformed payloads crashed the worker"_).
2. **Classic Style vs. Self-Conscious Metadiscourse (`pinker-2014`, `zinsser-1976`)**: Cut throat-clearing that narrates the writer's own act of writing (_"In this document, we will delve into..."_, _"Let's break this down"_, _"It is important to remember that"_). Point directly at the subject.
3. **Old-Before-New Cohesion and End-Focus (`williams-1981`)**: Begin sentences with concepts the reader already knows from the previous sentence, and place the new, complex, or emphatic information at the clause's end.
4. **Critique of Elegant Variation (`fowler-1926`)**: Do not rotate through approximate synonyms (_system $\to$ platform $\to$ ecosystem $\to$ paradigm_) merely to avoid repeating a noun. In technical and analytical prose, calling the same thing by the same name prevents confusion.

## 5. Synthesis of the Chinese Prose Tradition

Chinese prose (`rules/zh-CN.yaml`, `references/chinese.md`) is governed by its own typological structure:

1. **Verb-Centered Predication (`lv-zhu-1952`, `zhu-dexi-1982`)**: Chinese clauses thrive on agile verb predicates and serial verb constructions (连动句). Stripping omnipotent light verbs (`进行、加以、作出、予以、实现`) restores natural Chinese vigor (`对索引进行重建` $\to$ `重建索引`).
2. **Curing Malignant Europeanization (`wang-li-1943`, `yu-kwangchung-1987`)**: Replace heavy prepositional wrappers (`在……的背景下`, `通过……的方式`, `关于……的问题`), stacked `的` modifiers (`一个由多部门联合发起的针对……的方案`), and unnecessary `被` passives with compact chronological or topic-comment clauses.
3. **Collocation Integrity (`lv-zhu-1952`)**: Audit every verb–object (动宾) and modifier–head (偏正) pair against natural semantic selection (`培养……水平` $\to$ `提高……水平` or `培养……能力`; avoid pseudo-corporate mismatches like `对齐颗粒度`, `沉淀方法论`, `打通心智`).
4. **Cida (辞达) and Anti-Cliché Reflexes (`ye-shengtao-1934`, `zhu-guangqian-1946`)**: Reject both bureaucratic stock formulas (`官样套话`) and theatrical internet slang (`绝绝子、狠狠拿捏`). Read the sentence aloud: if it sounds like a rigid translation or a performative chat meme, rewrite it in clean modern standard Chinese.

## 6. Synthesis of the Japanese Prose Tradition

Japanese prose (`rules/ja.yaml`, `references/japanese.md`) relies on head-final syntax, pragmatic ellipsis, and explicit politeness morphology:

1. **Separation of Fact (`事実`) and Inference (`意見`) (`kinoshita-1981`)**: Japanese prose often blurs observation and conjecture at the sentence end (`〜という状況が見受けられるかと思われます`). State verified facts with definitive predicates (`〜である / 〜です`) and label inferences explicitly with their basis (`〜から、…と推測される`).
2. **Unambiguous Modifier Ordering (`honda-1976`, `iwabuchi-1960`)**: Because Japanese modifiers precede their heads, misplaced modifiers create _kakariuke_ ambiguity (`係り受けのねじれ`). Order modifiers from **long/clausal to short** and from **macro-context to micro-detail**, using commas (`、`) to mark structural boundaries.
3. **Register Consistency and Keigo Calibration (`bunkacho-2007`, `bunkacho-2022`)**: Maintain consistent `です・ます` (敬体) or `だ・である` (常体) throughout body prose. Eliminate double honorifics (`二重敬語`) and restrict `〜させていただく` to situations where the speaker genuinely requests permission and receives personal benefit.
4. **Trimming Sino-Japanese Light-Verb Padding and Translationese (`tanizaki-1934`, `iori-yasashii-2016`)**: Compress bloated noun-verb endings (`〜の実行を行う` $\to$ `〜を実行する`; `〜することが可能である` $\to$ `〜できる`), drop redundant third-person pronouns (`彼・彼女・それ`), and prefer settled Kango/Wago (`要件、課題、正常系`) over opaque Katakana jargon (`リクワイアメント、イシュー、ハッピーパス`).
